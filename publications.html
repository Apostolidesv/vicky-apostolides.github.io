Publications

My current writing projects focus on environmental microbiology, Appalachian ecosystems, karst environments, and water quality. The manuscripts listed below are works in progress.

Appalachian Microbial Communities: Geological Influences and Knowledge Gaps

Authors: Apostolides, V. and Washburn, R.

Status: In preparation

This literature review examines microbial communities across the Appalachian Mountains and explores how the region's geology, topography, and environmental conditions may contribute to microbial diversity. The project also identifies important gaps in current knowledge and potential directions for future research.

Human and Biological Impacts on Karst Systems: Emerging Microbial Knowledge Gaps

Authors: Apostolides, V. and Washburn, R.

Status: In preparation

This literature review investigates how urbanization, agriculture, tourism, wildlife, pollution, and other human and biological factors affect karst environments. It also highlights the limited understanding of how microbial communities influence karst processes and ecosystem health.

Spatial and Temporal Patterns of Escherichia coli Across the Kentucky River Basin

Authors: Apostolides, V., Washburn, R., et al.

Status: In preparation

This project uses long-term citizen-science data from Watershed Watch in Kentucky to examine spatial and temporal patterns in E. coli, rainfall, and turbidity across the Kentucky River Basin. QGIS was used to clean, organize, map, and visualize environmental conditions through monthly thematic map series.

Presentations

Vegetation Dynamics and Stability of Alaskan Proglacial Braidplains

Presenter: Victoria Apostolides

Research advisor: Dr. William Armstrong

Year: 2026

This presentation examines vegetation establishment and landscape stability across Alaskan proglacial braidplains using Landsat imagery, NDVI, NDWI, and multidecadal geospatial analysis.

Drone-Based Assessment of Riparian Vegetation Health

Presenter: Victoria Apostolides

Research mentor: Dr. Michael Sama

Venue: University of Kentucky NSF REU Program

Year: 2026

This methods-focused presentation describes the use of drone photogrammetry, RGB vegetation indices, Random Forest classification, and SAM2 segmentation to compare vegetation conditions along natural and human-impacted streams.

Flood Modeling and Hydrologic Analysis of a Central Kentucky Catchment

Presenter: Victoria Apostolides

Research mentor: Dr. Meredith Swallom

Organization: Kentucky Geological Survey

Year: 2025

This project used Mesonet and NOAA data, GIS, Python, and TUFLOW to investigate precipitation patterns, catchment hydrology, and flood behavior in central Kentucky.
